▲ TWO REAL CAVEATS, STATED UP FRONT

The nRF24 chip-select pins are triple-booked with UART0 and the external header, so serial logging and 2.4 GHz traffic can interfere, and LilyGO's own issue tracker notes some Plus units losing nRF range over time. And because the SD card, the CC1101 and the nRF24 all share one SPI bus with the display, every driver has to cooperate on that bus rather than assume it owns it — a lesson this port learned the hard way. The sharpest example: the CC1101’s SO pin is also its GDO1, so unless it is left three-stated it drives that shared MISO line even while its own chip-select is idle — quietly corrupting every nRF24 read, pulling one-bits down while zero-bits stayed clean. It presented as flaky hardware for weeks. It was one register.

Neither wins outright. The Cardputer keeps a real keyboard and reaches LoRa, GNSS and wired Ethernet through hats. The T-Embed folds sub-GHz, 2.4 GHz, NFC and IR onto one board and adds a fuel gauge. Pick the body the mission wants.
